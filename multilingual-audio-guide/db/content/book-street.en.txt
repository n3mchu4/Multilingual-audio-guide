Located right next to the Saigon Central Post Office, Nguyen Van Binh Street is short yet possesses a distinct cultural atmosphere: this is Ho Chi Minh City Book Street, inaugurated and opened on January 9, 2016, to celebrate the New Year and look forward to Vietnam Book Day[cite: 20]. It is the first well-planned and structured book street in Vietnam, developed by transforming a public space with a history spanning several decades[cite: 20].

Measuring approximately 144 meters in length, the brick-paved street is shaded by lush trees and hosts about 20 permanent book stalls operated by major domestic publishers and distributors, alongside secondhand and rare book booths, book cafes, and spaces for exhibitions, author meet-and-greets, and book launches[cite: 20]. The architecture of the stalls is uniformly designed to harmonize with the surrounding landscape of Notre-Dame Cathedral and the Central Post Office, creating a unified historical and cultural complex in the heart of the city[cite: 20].

Since its opening, Nguyen Van Binh Book Street has quickly become a familiar cultural gathering spot for city residents, especially youth, students, and book lovers[cite: 20]. It frequently hosts events such as book exhibitions, reading skill workshops, author exchanges with domestic and international writers, as well as activities celebrating Vietnam Book and Reading Culture Day (April 21) and World Book and Copyright Day[cite: 20].

More than just a place to buy and sell books, the Book Street is regarded as a "cultural lung" in the urban center, where visitors can stroll, rest, enjoy coffee, and explore books about Saigon's history and culture past and present[cite: 20]. With its prime location between two iconic architectural landmarks—Notre-Dame Cathedral and the Central Post Office—Nguyen Van Binh Book Street enriches the cultural experience of tourists exploring downtown, while demonstrating efforts to preserve and spread reading culture in modern urban life[cite: 20].
